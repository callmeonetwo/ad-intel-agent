# AI tools and apps (US): competitor creative report

*Snapshot 2026-10-22 · 120 active ads from 38 advertisers · built by Ad Intel Agent*

**In the US AI-app niche, the ads that have likely paid off longest are plain static images and clear, specific promises, while templated catalog and dynamic ads are mostly newer and not yet proven.**

- All 120 ads are from direct competitors (38 brands) and all are still running. Median days live is 63.5, and 35 ads have run 90+ days.
- Static IMAGE ads have the longest median run (224 days, n=24), then VIDEO (79 days, n=40). DCO (dynamic ads) is the biggest group (55 ads) but has a median of 29 days, so it is newer and not yet proven.
- One brand, Nexus, makes 35 of the 120 ads from just 5 unique copies. This skews the totals, so the long-runner patterns are the better guide.

## Key findings

### 1. Static images run the longest
Image ads have the highest median days live of any display format. Advertisers likely keep them on because they work. Note that 13 of the 24 image ads come from one brand, SmithBook.

*Evidence:* IMAGE median 224 days (n=24); VIDEO 79 (n=40); DCO 29 (n=55). SmithBook: 13 image ads, median 224 days, max 255.

### 2. Templated, dynamic ads dominate the newer ads
Dynamic creative and template copy are rare among 90+ day runners but common among newer ads. They are numerous, but they are newer and not yet proven. Much of this comes from Nexus (94.3% template copy).

*Evidence:* DCO: 14.3% of 90+ day ads vs 58.8% of newer ads. Template copy: 8.6% vs 75.3%. Nexus: 35 ads, 5 unique copies, median 29 days.

### 3. Offers like free trials are common but not tied to longevity
Free trials appear in 46 ads, but their median is 29 days, so they are mostly newer ads. Ads with no offer have a median of 84 days. Guarantee, lead magnet and discount show high medians but have only 2-3 ads each, so they are directional only.

*Evidence:* free_trial n=46, median 29; none n=66, median 84; guarantee n=2, median 340; lead_magnet n=3, median 227; discount n=2, median 140. Any offer: 25.7% of 90+ day ads vs 52.9% of newer.

### 4. Statement hooks are the default; rarer hooks have longer runs
Most ads open with a plain statement. Story, question, promise and shock hooks have higher medians, but the groups are small. Treat these as directional.

*Evidence:* statement n=53, median 30; promise n=12, median 114; question n=6, median 123; shock n=4, median 129; story n=3, median 180; benefit_list n=3, median 142.

### 5. App demos and graphics outlast catalog ads
Graphic ads and app demos have longer medians than catalog-style ads. Catalog ads are the largest group (57) but newer. UGC and talking-head groups are too small to judge.

*Evidence:* graphic n=24, median 224; app_demo n=22, median 82; product_video n=8, median 104; catalog n=57, median 29; ugc n=4, median 46; talking_head n=3, median 128.

### 6. Social proof is more common in long-running ads
Social proof appears more in 90+ day ads than in newer ones, though overall use is low. It is a likely signal, not proof.

*Evidence:* Social proof: 20.0% of 90+ day ads vs 8.2% of newer ads; 11.7% overall. Urgency: 5.7% vs 9.4%; 8.3% overall.

## Who sells how

- **Nexus - AI Video Generator**: Runs high-volume, templated free-trial ads for AI video (35 ads, 5 unique copies), mostly dynamic creative with an install push.
- **SmithBook**: Sells AI book writing with branded static images and varied copy (11 unique copies across 13 ads), no offer, median 224 days live.
- **Picshiner - Photo AI Editor**: Rides the AI photo-trend angle with templated dynamic ads, all 7 ads at about 21 days live (newer).
- **AI Photo Editor**: Mostly video (6 of 7 ads) on niche use cases like product photos and haircut try-on, with varied hooks.
- **Jenni AI**: AI research assistant using social proof and free-trial offers in dynamic ads, median 48 days.
- **Francis Hughes**: Pushes AI photo editing through video with no hook or offer (5 ads, 1 unique copy), median 86 days.
- **CoolDeep.AI**: Offers a free download lead magnet in static images, median 227 days (3 ads, directional).
- **Vid.ai**: Sells income from faceless videos with a feature list in static image ads, median about 248 days (2 ads, directional).

## Gaps nobody is using well

- UGC, talking-head and influencer formats are barely used (4, 3 and 1 ads), so authentic human-voice creative is largely open.
- Social proof appears in only 11.7% of ads, although it is more common in 90+ day runners.
- Hooks beyond plain statements (question, story, shock) are rare, with 6, 3 and 4 ads respectively.

## Recommendations: what to make next

### 1. Launch a static image set with specific outcome promises (start now)
**Make:** 6-8 static 1:1 and 4:5 images, each showing one before/after result from the app with a one-line promise and a clear install CTA. Vary the copy across the set rather than repeating one line.

**Why:** IMAGE has the longest median days live (224, n=24). SmithBook's 13 images use 11 unique copies and run a median of 224 days. This is a proxy, not proof, and it is skewed by one brand.

**Example hook:** "Turn a rough draft into a finished page in one tap."

### 2. Make a 20-30 second app demo video (start now)
**Make:** Screen recording of the core flow, from input to result, with captions and a result shown in the first 3 seconds. Make 2-3 cuts with different openers.

**Why:** app_demo is the second-largest non-catalog group (n=22, median 82 days) and VIDEO has a median of 79 days (n=40). This is a solid middle ground between static and dynamic ads.

**Example hook:** "Watch this photo go from dull to done in 10 seconds."

### 3. Test promise and question hooks (test)
**Make:** Two sets of the same demo video: one opening with a bold, specific promise, and one opening with a question about the viewer's problem.

**Why:** Promise (n=12, median 114 days) and question (n=6, median 123) outlast statement (n=53, median 30). The groups are small, so treat this as a test.

**Example hook:** "What if your next video took 2 minutes, not 2 hours?"

### 4. Add a social proof variant (test)
**Make:** A static or video ad built around a real user quote, download count or rating. Use only real, checkable claims.

**Why:** Social proof is in 20.0% of 90+ day ads vs 8.2% of newer ads, and only 11.7% of ads overall, so it is underused.

**Example hook:** ""I finished my first video in 5 minutes." Real user, real result."

### 5. Try a UGC-style creator video (test)
**Make:** A 15-25 second selfie-style video of a real person using the app, with a problem, a demo and an install CTA.

**Why:** UGC, talking-head and influencer ads total only 8 of 120, so there is open space. The talking-head median is 128 days (n=3) and UGC is 46 (n=4), too small to judge. UGC is newer and not yet proven here.

**Example hook:** "I tried every AI photo app so you don't have to."

### 6. Pick a clear, specific use-case angle (start now)
**Make:** Build each ad set around one use case, such as AI video creation, photo editing, book writing or a trend, instead of a generic AI claim.

**Why:** The top angles are AI video creation (18 ads), AI photo editing (12) and free download (10). Niche angles like book creation also appear, with SmithBook running long.

**Example hook:** "Write your book outline in minutes, not months."

### 7. Hold off on a big dynamic or catalog template push (later)
**Make:** Keep the first budget on unique creative. Run a small dynamic test only after you have winning images and videos to feed it.

**Why:** DCO has a median of 29 days (n=55) and appears in 58.8% of newer ads vs 14.3% of 90+ day ads. These ads are newer and not yet proven, and Nexus accounts for 31 of the 55 DCO ads.

**Example hook:** "Use your best-performing image hook as the dynamic headline."

## Limits of this data

- Days live is a proxy for profit, not proof. Every ad is still running, so short runs mean newer, not failed.
- The data has no spend, CTR or ROAS figures, so we cannot say which ads actually performed best.
- Nexus makes 35 of 120 ads and SmithBook 13, so a few brands skew the format and hook totals.
- Many groups are small (n<5: story, benefit_list, shock, social_proof, UGC, talking head, guarantee, lead magnet, discount), so those patterns are directional only. No reels data was provided.

## Method

Apify collected public ads from the Meta Ad Library and public reels from Instagram. Claude Haiku tagged every creative (hook, promise, angle, emotion, format, proof, urgency, offer, CTA) as JSON; Claude Sonnet wrote the findings from counted statistics only. Full tagged table: `ai_tools_us_creatives.csv`. Model usage for this run: labeling 120 creatives with Haiku: 6 calls, $0.007 (cached after the first run); report with Sonnet: $0.056.
